Car Hauling Insurance

A guide to auto transport insurance questions, from truck and trailer protection to customer vehicle values and pickup-to-delivery responsibilities.

Insurance guide · Updated November 2, 2026 · By Motive Insurance

When you haul a vehicle, you are responsible for more than your truck and trailer.

You are also responsible for somebody else's automobile.

One load may represent tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars in customer property.

This guide explains the differences between commercial auto, cargo, equipment, and business liability considerations for vehicle transport operations.

What is car hauling insurance?

Car hauling insurance is commercial insurance structured around businesses transporting automobiles and other vehicles.

Operations may include:

  • Single-car hauling
  • Multi-car hauling
  • Auto transport
  • Dealer vehicle transport
  • Auction transport
  • Vehicle relocation
  • Enclosed vehicle transport
  • Open vehicle transport
  • Classic car transport
  • Luxury vehicle transport
  • Fleet vehicle relocation
  • Interstate auto transport

Each operation should be evaluated based on what is being transported and how.

Commercial auto liability

The power unit is still a commercial vehicle.

Commercial auto liability can help address covered bodily injury and property damage resulting from the operation of insured commercial vehicles.

The truck, trailer, driver, routes and operating radius should all be represented accurately.

Motor truck cargo for vehicle transporters

Cargo coverage is a major consideration for car haulers because the cargo is the customer's vehicle.

The value of a load can increase quickly.

Consider a trailer carrying:

  • Several late-model vehicles
  • Luxury vehicles
  • Classic automobiles
  • Dealer inventory
  • Specialty vehicles

A cargo limit that appears adequate for one type of load may be insufficient for another.

Car haulers should evaluate:

  • Maximum number of vehicles per load
  • Average vehicle value
  • Maximum vehicle value
  • Maximum total load value
  • Open versus enclosed transport
  • Loading procedures
  • Securement
  • Storage
  • Routes
  • Deductibles
  • Cargo exclusions

The cargo limit should reflect real operations—not an arbitrary number chosen only to satisfy a certificate requirement.

Physical damage for the truck and trailer

Cargo coverage protects a different interest than physical damage coverage.

Physical damage can help protect insured trucks and trailers against covered collision and comprehensive losses.

A complete review should consider the value of:

  • Power unit
  • Trailer
  • Specialized equipment
  • Aftermarket additions

Loading and unloading exposure

Car hauling risk does not begin once the truck reaches highway speed.

Damage can happen while a vehicle is:

  • Driven onto the trailer
  • Positioned
  • Secured
  • Moved between decks
  • Unloaded
  • Transferred
  • Stored temporarily

Loading procedures, driver experience and equipment condition all matter.

Open vs. enclosed car hauling

Open car carriers and enclosed transporters can have different exposures.

Enclosed transport is often used for:

  • Luxury automobiles
  • Classic vehicles
  • Exotic vehicles
  • Collector cars
  • Specialty vehicles

Higher individual vehicle values should be discussed with the insurer.

Do not assume a standard cargo limit automatically matches a high-value auto transport operation.

Dealer and auction vehicle transport

Car haulers may move vehicles for:

  • Auto dealerships
  • Dealer groups
  • Auto auctions
  • Fleet operators
  • Rental companies
  • Manufacturers
  • Private customers

Commercial contracts may establish their own insurance requirements.

Compare requested limits and certificates with the actual loads, contracts, and policy wording.

General liability

Some vehicle transport businesses also have premises, loading areas or other operations outside normal highway transportation.

General liability may help address certain covered third-party business claims not arising from auto liability.

Workers' compensation

Drivers and employees may be injured while loading, securing, inspecting or transporting vehicles.

Workers' compensation should be considered based on staffing and applicable state requirements.

Umbrella or excess liability

Larger auto transport operations and certain contracts may require higher liability limits.

Umbrella or excess liability may be appropriate depending on the underlying coverage and business requirements.

Organize the following information before comparing coverage:

  • Number of trucks

  • Truck values
  • Trailer values
  • Driver information
  • USDOT information when applicable
  • Operating radius
  • States of operation
  • Types of vehicles transported
  • Maximum vehicle value

  • Average number of vehicles per load
  • Open or enclosed transport
  • Customers and contracts

  • Current insurance
  • Requested cargo limit
  • Renewal date

Starting a car hauling business?

Do not build the business first and ask about insurance at the last minute.

Before accepting your first transport job, understand:

  • What truck you will operate
  • What trailer you will use
  • What vehicles you will transport
  • Where you will operate
  • How much cargo value you may carry
  • What contracts require
  • Which filings or authority may apply to your operation
  • What information insurers will request

Insurance is part of the operating model.

Resolve the insurance questions alongside equipment, operating authority, and contract planning before accepting a transport assignment.

From one trailer to a car-hauling fleet

Growth creates new complexity.

Additional trucks mean additional drivers.

Additional trailers mean additional values.

Larger contracts can increase cargo concentration.

Longer routes increase mileage.

Higher-end clients can increase vehicle values.

A growing car hauling business should review insurance as those variables change.

Protect the equipment and the vehicles you are paid to move

The truck matters.

The trailer matters.

The driver matters.

The customer's vehicle matters.

Car hauling insurance should account for all four.

Compare coverage against the actual equipment, routes, vehicle values, and responsibilities in the operation.

A practical example

Example: calculate the total value on one load

For illustration, six customer vehicles valued at $40,000 each create $240,000 in combined vehicle value. That arithmetic is a starting point for a cargo-limit discussion, not a recommended limit or a promise of payment. Check any per-vehicle restrictions, valuation terms, deductibles, exclusions, and maximum exposure on other loads. A load with fewer luxury vehicles can be worth more than a full load of lower-value vehicles.

Common mistakes to avoid

  1. Confusing a filing requirement with cargo protection

    Regulatory filings and protection for customer vehicles answer different questions. Review the applicable rules alongside the cargo policy and customer contracts.

  2. Using an average load instead of a maximum load

    Track the highest reasonably expected combined vehicle value and any concentration of expensive vehicles. Revisit that figure when accepting new customers or equipment.

  3. Assuming loading and temporary storage are automatically included

    Read when coverage begins and ends. Identify who loads, unloads, transfers, or stores vehicles and ask how each activity is treated.

Frequently asked questions

What insurance does a car hauler need?

Depending on the operation, a car hauler may need commercial auto liability, motor truck cargo, physical damage, general liability, workers' compensation and additional coverage.

Does cargo insurance cover cars being transported?

Motor truck cargo insurance may provide coverage for customer vehicles being transported, subject to the policy's terms, conditions, limits, deductibles and exclusions.

How much cargo insurance does a car hauler need?

There is no single appropriate limit for every operation. Consider the maximum number of vehicles hauled and the maximum combined value that could be on one load.

Does enclosed auto transport require different insurance?

The fundamental coverage categories may be similar, but enclosed carriers frequently transport higher-value vehicles. Vehicle values and maximum cargo exposure should be discussed with the insurer.

What should a new car hauling business review first?

Start with the truck, trailer, expected loads, customer contracts, operating area, and driver experience. Work out the maximum total vehicle value on one load and ask how the policy addresses loading, unloading, storage, and exclusions.
