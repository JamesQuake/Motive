The whole operation

Towing Creates More Than One Insurance Risk

A towing company does more than operate a commercial truck.

Your business may pick up disabled vehicles, transport customer vehicles, perform roadside assistance, recover damaged vehicles, store vehicles, work with motor clubs, or participate in police and municipal towing programs.

Each part of that operation creates a different exposure. That is why tow truck insurance should be reviewed around what your business actually does—not simply the vehicle you drive.

Car Hauling or Towing — What's the Difference?

You may hear the terms car hauling, vehicle transport, and towing used to describe similar types of work.

Most customers simply call it towing. In the insurance industry, car hauling or vehicle transportation may also be used when describing businesses that move customer vehicles.

Whether you operate a tow truck, rollback, flatbed, or vehicle transporter, the important question is how your business handles the customer's vehicle—and what coverage applies while that vehicle is being transported or stored.

Coverage areas

What Insurance Should a Towing Business Review?

The appropriate coverage depends on your trucks, drivers, services, operating area, customer vehicles, storage operations, and contracts.

Commercial Auto Liability

Commercial auto liability can address covered bodily injury or property damage claims arising from operation of insured commercial vehicles.

Physical Damage

Physical damage can help protect an insured tow truck, rollback, or wrecker against covered collision and comprehensive losses, subject to policy terms and deductibles.

On-Hook Coverage

On-hook coverage is designed to address covered damage to a customer's vehicle while it is being towed or transported by the towing operation.

Garagekeepers Coverage

Garagekeepers coverage addresses customer vehicles being stored or kept in the towing company's care at its location, subject to policy terms.

General Liability

General liability can address certain covered third-party bodily injury or property damage exposures arising from business activities outside normal vehicle operation.

Workers' Compensation

Workers' compensation should be reviewed based on employees, business structure, job duties, and applicable requirements.

Follow the vehicle

Where Is the Customer's Vehicle?

One of the easiest ways to understand towing insurance is to follow the customer's vehicle through the operation.

  1. 01

    Your tow truck

    Commercial Auto + Physical Damage

  2. 02

    Customer vehicle is loaded and transported

    On-Hook Coverage

  3. 03

    Customer vehicle is stored at your location

    Garagekeepers Coverage

The tow truck and the customer's vehicle are not the same insurance exposure.

A towing company that transports vehicles and stores them should review both parts of that risk.

Illinois registration

Illinois Tow Truck Insurance Requirements

Illinois establishes minimum insurance requirements for tow-truck registration.

Illinois tow-truck registration insurance minimums
CoverageIllinois Registration Minimum
Automobile Liability$500,000 CSL
Garage Liability$500,000 CSL
On-Hook Coverage$25,000
Garagekeepers Coverage$25,000
Workers' CompensationWhen applicable

CSL means combined single limit. Source: Illinois Secretary of State tow-truck registration guidance (PDF).

Certain exceptions can apply depending on the operation. These amounts are registration minimums—not necessarily the amount of insurance every towing company should carry.

Illinois minimums do not automatically equal your contract requirements or your actual business exposure.

2026 Illinois Update

Illinois strengthened portions of its commercial towing enforcement framework in 2026. Towing companies should verify the current registration, Illinois Commerce Commission, local, contractual, and insurance requirements that apply to their specific operation.

On-Hook vs. Garagekeepers: What's the Difference?

On-Hook

Customer vehicle is being:

  • Loaded
  • Towed
  • Carried
  • Transported

Think: the customer's vehicle is moving with you.

Garagekeepers

Customer vehicle is:

  • Stored
  • Held
  • Parked at the towing facility
  • In the company's care at its location

Think: the customer's vehicle is staying with you.

Transporting → Review on-hookStoring → Review garagekeepers

If your business does both, your insurance review should address both exposures.

Loading, unloading and storage protection depend on the policy wording, limits and exclusions. Confirm when each coverage applies.

Before you sign

Minimum Coverage May Not Satisfy Every Towing Contract

A towing company can meet a state registration requirement and still need higher insurance limits for certain work.

  • Police rotation programs
  • Municipalities
  • Motor clubs
  • Auto dealerships
  • Repair facilities
  • Commercial fleets
  • Property managers
  • Government agencies

Some towing arrangements can require insurance limits above the basic tow-truck registration minimums.

Before signing a towing contract, review its insurance requirements, including applicable liability limits, on-hook limits, garagekeepers requirements, general liability, workers' compensation, certificates of insurance, and additional insured requirements.

The legal minimum is not always the contractual minimum.
