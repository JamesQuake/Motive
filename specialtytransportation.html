Understand the main coverage categories.

Use these coverage definitions as a starting point, then explore the guide for your operation. Actual policy terms and business activities determine what applies.

Commercial Auto Liability

Helps protect your business when covered vehicles are involved in accidents that cause injury or property damage.

Physical Damage

Helps protect business vehicles from covered losses such as collision, theft, vandalism, fire, and weather damage.

Passenger Liability

Important for businesses that transport people, including medical transportation, shuttle, church, daycare, limousine, and private services.

General Liability

Helps protect against common business claims that may happen outside normal vehicle operation.

Workers Compensation

Helps protect employees who are injured on the job and supports employer protection where required.

Cargo / Vehicle Transport Coverage

For companies responsible for vehicles, equipment, or property while in care, custody, or transit.

Risk Review

Why specialty transportation businesses need the right insurance.

Specialty transportation businesses are exposed to more than ordinary commercial auto risk. Your operation may involve passengers, contracts, schedules, drivers, vehicle care, medical transportation requirements, or roadside service calls.

How to use these transportation guides.

Start with your operation’s guide, compare the coverage categories, and use its document checklist to identify questions. The examples explain how a review works; they do not determine coverage or promise an insurance placement.

What shapes your coverage?

  • What you transport
  • Where your vehicles operate
  • How many drivers you use
  • Whether passengers are involved
  • Whether vehicles are in your care
  • Contract or compliance requirements
FAQs

Specialty Transportation Insurance Questions

Specialty transportation includes operations such as non-emergency medical transportation, wheelchair transportation, towing, roadside assistance, passenger shuttle services, and vehicle transportation.

Each business carries different risks involving passengers, vehicles, property, or equipment. Review those operational differences alongside the policy wording and applicable requirements.

NEMT businesses should review commercial auto liability, passenger exposures, general liability, and potentially other coverage related to their services.

Vehicle accessibility equipment, passenger assistance, driver qualifications, and transportation contracts may affect coverage requirements. These operational details should be documented when reviewing policy terms and contracts.

Towing businesses should review commercial auto coverage and protection for customers' vehicles while being towed or held in the business's care.

On-hook towing and garagekeepers coverage are examples of specialized protections that may be relevant. Read the policy definitions, covered activities, and custody arrangements to understand the differences.

Transporting passengers introduces risks and insurance requirements that differ from transporting merchandise or equipment.

Passenger capacity, vehicle configuration, transportation contracts, and applicable regulations can influence the type and amount of coverage required. Check the actual passenger operation, vehicle configuration, contracts, and applicable requirements together.
