Shuttle Insurance

Understand the coverage questions behind airport, hotel, employee, church, daycare, and group passenger transportation.

Insurance guide · Updated November 2, 2026 · By Motive Insurance

Airport shuttle.

Hotel shuttle.

Employee transportation.

Church transportation.

Daycare transportation.

Private group transportation.

The destination may change, but one thing does not:

Your business is responsible for passengers while they are being transported.

This guide explains how vehicles, seating capacity, drivers, routes, contracts, and passenger responsibilities shape a shuttle insurance review.

Shuttle operations are different from ordinary commercial auto

A commercial van used by a contractor to carry equipment does not create the same exposure as a van carrying passengers.

For shuttle companies, the people inside the vehicle are a central part of the risk.

Insurance considerations may include:

  • Vehicle seating capacity
  • Number of passengers
  • Type of passengers
  • Driver qualifications
  • Frequency of trips
  • Regular routes
  • Airport operations
  • Hotel contracts
  • Employee transportation contracts
  • Church transportation
  • Daycare transportation
  • Interstate operations
  • Vehicle ownership

That makes shuttle insurance a specialty transportation conversation.

Airport shuttle insurance

Airport shuttle businesses may operate on tight schedules with frequent pickups and drop-offs.

Vehicles may spend significant time:

  • In airport traffic
  • At terminals
  • At hotels
  • On highways
  • In parking and loading zones
  • Transporting luggage
  • Carrying multiple passengers

The insurance review should reflect the frequency and nature of those operations rather than simply classifying the vehicle as a van or bus.

Hotel shuttle insurance

Hotels and independent transportation providers may offer scheduled or on-demand shuttle service between hotels, airports, entertainment areas and other destinations.

When transportation is provided as part of a larger service or commercial arrangement, operators should understand how that transportation is classified and what insurance requirements apply.

Document the vehicle use and passenger responsibilities associated with the service, including who owns the vehicle and who employs the driver.

Employee transportation insurance

Businesses sometimes transport workers between:

  • Parking areas and job sites
  • Facilities
  • Warehouses
  • Hotels
  • Construction projects
  • Factories
  • Airports
  • Transit stations
  • Remote work locations

Employee shuttle operations may involve repeated routes and high passenger frequency.

Coverage should be evaluated around the actual transportation arrangement, vehicle capacity and operation.

Church and group transportation

Churches, nonprofit organizations and community groups may operate vans or buses for events, services and group activities.

Nonprofit status by itself does not eliminate transportation risk.

The insurance review should consider who owns the vehicle, who drives it, how transportation is funded, seating capacity, where trips occur and how frequently passengers are transported.

Daycare and child transportation

Transporting children creates responsibilities that should be evaluated carefully.

Daycare transportation may involve:

  • Pickup and drop-off
  • Scheduled routes
  • Multiple stops
  • Passenger supervision
  • Vans or buses
  • Special loading procedures
  • Contracts or licensing requirements

Insurance requirements can vary by jurisdiction and operation.

Review the vehicle use, passenger supervision, and written transportation responsibilities together.

Commercial auto and passenger liability

Shuttle insurance often begins with commercial auto liability.

But passenger transportation requires a deeper review than simply listing vehicles.

For qualifying interstate for-hire passenger carriers, federal financial responsibility requirements can depend on the highest seating capacity of vehicles in the fleet.

Operators should also consider applicable state requirements and contractual limits.

Verify which rules apply to the actual operation before treating a regulatory minimum as the right limit for the business.

Physical damage

Your shuttle vehicles generate revenue.

Physical damage coverage can help protect insured vehicles against covered collision and comprehensive losses.

For larger fleets, vehicle values, deductibles and replacement considerations should be reviewed together rather than one vehicle at a time.

General liability

A shuttle company also has business exposures away from the driver's seat.

General liability may help address certain covered claims involving business operations that fall outside normal commercial auto liability.

Workers' compensation

Drivers and other employees may face injuries while performing their jobs.

Workers' compensation requirements vary, but it should be considered as part of the overall insurance program.

What shapes shuttle insurance cost?

Pricing may be influenced by:

  • Passenger capacity
  • Number of vehicles
  • Vehicle type
  • Vehicle value
  • Driver records
  • Years in business
  • Claims history
  • Routes
  • Annual mileage
  • Operating radius
  • Interstate travel
  • Airport exposure
  • Contracts
  • Passenger type
  • Coverage limits
  • Deductibles

Build insurance around the route

The right question is not:

  • “What does shuttle insurance cost?”
  • It is:
  • “What does this shuttle operation actually do?”

A three-vehicle hotel shuttle serving one airport has a different risk profile from an employee transportation company operating twenty vehicles across several states.

Start with an accurate description of the operation.

Then compare the insurance documents with that description.

A practical example

Example: a hotel shuttle begins employee transportation

A hotel’s shuttle begins carrying a nearby business’s staff during shift changes. The vehicle may be unchanged, but the passengers, frequency, contract, and responsibility for transportation have changed. Update the route description and gather the new agreement before assuming the original insurance arrangement fits the additional work. The example is a planning prompt, not a coverage determination.

Common mistakes to avoid

  1. Using today’s passenger count as the seating capacity

    A partially occupied van can still have the same designed seating configuration. Record the actual vehicle configuration instead of describing only the number of riders on a typical trip.

  2. Assuming a free ride has no insurance implications

    Transportation offered as part of another business service still needs an accurate description. Document how the service is arranged and discuss how that arrangement affects the review.

  3. Ignoring the driver arrangement

    Employees, volunteers, and contracted transportation providers can create different operational structures. Keep the vehicle owner and driver employer clear.

Frequently asked questions

What insurance does a shuttle business need?

Coverage may include commercial auto liability, physical damage, general liability, workers' compensation, umbrella or excess liability and other coverage depending on the operation.

Is airport shuttle insurance different from regular commercial auto?

It can be. Passenger transportation, airport operations, routes, vehicle capacity and contractual requirements can create considerations not present in a standard commercial vehicle account.

Does seating capacity affect shuttle insurance?

Yes. Passenger capacity can affect underwriting and may affect regulatory financial responsibility requirements for certain operations.

What makes employee shuttle transportation different?

Identify the vehicle owner, driver employer, passengers, routes, and transportation contract. A service carrying another company’s employees can have a different structure from an employer moving its own workers.

Can a new shuttle company get insurance?

Potentially. Available options depend on the vehicles, drivers, routes, business plan, experience and insurance market.
