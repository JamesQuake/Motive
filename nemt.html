NEMT Insurance

A practical guide to insurance considerations for medical transportation vehicles, passengers, drivers, accessibility equipment, and contracts.

Insurance guide · Updated November 2, 2026 · By Motive Insurance

Non-emergency medical transportation businesses do more than move passengers from one address to another.

Your drivers may transport seniors, Medicaid members, passengers using wheelchairs, people traveling to dialysis or therapy appointments, and individuals who need additional assistance entering or exiting a vehicle.

That creates a different risk profile than an ordinary commercial vehicle.

This guide explains the coverage categories, operational details, and contract questions that belong in an NEMT insurance review.

Commercial auto. Passenger exposure. Wheelchair transportation. Drivers. Contracts. Business liability.

One insurance conversation.

What is NEMT insurance?

NEMT insurance is commercial insurance structured around businesses providing non-emergency medical transportation.

It may apply to operations such as:

  • Non-emergency medical transportation
  • Wheelchair transportation
  • Ambulette services
  • Senior transportation
  • Medicaid transportation
  • Dialysis transportation
  • Medical appointment transportation
  • Mobility-assisted transportation
  • Private-pay medical transportation

The appropriate insurance program depends on the vehicles you use, who you transport, where you operate, how drivers are hired, whether passengers require assistance, and the contracts your company accepts.

That is why an NEMT operation should not be treated like a generic commercial auto account.

What insurance does an NEMT business need?

Commercial Auto Liability

Commercial auto liability is a core component of most NEMT insurance programs.

It can help address covered bodily injury or property damage claims resulting from the operation of insured vehicles.

The vehicles, passenger capacity, operating radius, driver history and use of each vehicle can all affect the insurance review.

Physical Damage

Your vehicles are essential business assets.

Physical damage coverage can help protect insured vehicles against covered collision and comprehensive losses such as theft, vandalism, fire, weather damage and other covered events.

Passenger Exposure

NEMT businesses carry people.

That distinction matters.

Passengers may have mobility limitations, use wheelchairs or other equipment, or require assistance entering and exiting a vehicle.

An insurance review should account for the passenger exposure created by the services the business actually provides.

General Liability

Not every business claim begins with a vehicle accident.

General liability can help address certain covered third-party bodily injury or property damage claims arising from business operations outside the normal commercial auto exposure.

Workers' Compensation

Drivers and transportation staff can face workplace injuries while assisting passengers, operating accessibility equipment, entering facilities, loading mobility devices or performing other duties.

Workers' compensation should be reviewed based on the company's employees, state requirements and operational structure.

Umbrella or Excess Liability

Some NEMT operations, contracts or larger fleets may need liability limits beyond the underlying policies.

Umbrella or excess liability may provide additional protection above certain eligible underlying liability policies.

Wheelchair transportation changes the risk

A wheelchair-accessible transportation company is not simply operating a van with more equipment.

The operation may involve:

  • Wheelchair ramps
  • Hydraulic lifts
  • Securement systems
  • Passenger restraints
  • Mobility devices
  • Driver assistance
  • Loading and unloading passengers
  • Specialized vehicle modifications

The condition and use of this equipment can become part of the overall risk.

Map the entire passenger journey, from approaching the vehicle to arriving at the destination. Identify who assists the passenger, which equipment is used, and where responsibility changes hands.

Medicaid and contract transportation

Some NEMT providers perform trips through Medicaid transportation programs, transportation brokers, healthcare networks, facilities or other contractual arrangements.

Those contracts can establish insurance requirements that go beyond simply having a commercial auto policy.

Your operation may need to provide certificates of insurance or demonstrate particular liability limits before a contract can be activated or renewed.

Requirements can vary by state, program, broker and contract.

Compare each written insurance requirement with the declarations, coverage forms, and endorsements in the actual policy. A certificate alone does not resolve every coverage question.

What affects the cost of NEMT insurance?

NEMT insurance pricing is not determined by one factor.

Insurers may consider:

  • Number of vehicles
  • Vehicle type
  • Passenger capacity
  • Vehicle age and value
  • Wheelchair accessibility
  • Operating radius
  • States of operation
  • Number of drivers
  • Driver experience
  • Motor vehicle records
  • Loss history
  • Annual mileage
  • Type of passengers transported
  • Contracts
  • Hours of operation
  • Business experience
  • Coverage limits

The goal should not be to find an insurance policy based on one generic price.

The goal is to build an NEMT insurance program that reflects the operation being insured.

Starting an NEMT business?

Insurance should be part of the business planning process.

Before accepting transportation contracts, understand what your vehicles will be used for and what coverage those contracts may require.

Questions to document before reviewing coverage:

  • What type of passengers will you transport?
  • Will you provide wheelchair transportation?
  • How many vehicles will you operate?
  • What vehicles are you purchasing?
  • How many passengers can each vehicle carry?
  • Will transportation be private-pay, Medicaid, facility-based or contract work?
  • How far will your vehicles travel?
  • Will you cross state lines?
  • Who will drive?
  • Do your drivers assist passengers?
  • Do you currently have contracts requiring specific insurance limits?

Those answers help move the conversation from “I need commercial auto insurance” to a real NEMT insurance review.

Growing NEMT fleets

A transportation company with one vehicle has a different insurance challenge from a company operating fifteen.

Growth can add:

  • More vehicles
  • More drivers
  • More passengers
  • More facilities
  • More contracts
  • Higher mileage
  • Additional certificates
  • More claims exposure
  • Additional management responsibilities

Insurance should be reviewed as the operation changes.

Compare the current operation with the description used when the policies were issued. A new service area, additional passenger assistance, or a larger fleet can change the questions that need to be reviewed.

Key takeaways for NEMT operators

Your passengers depend on your business to get them where they need to go.

Your business depends on the vehicles and people that make those trips possible.

A useful review connects the coverage documents to the entire transportation operation.

A practical example

Example: a wheelchair-accessible van joins the fleet

An operator adds an accessible van to a fleet previously used for ambulatory passengers. Listing the vehicle is only one step. Record the lift or ramp, securement equipment, passenger assistance tasks, seating configuration, and any new facility agreement. Ask how each change is reflected in the policy. This example illustrates a review process; it does not establish whether a particular loss would be covered.

Common mistakes to avoid

  1. Confusing the passenger benefit with business insurance

    Medicaid transportation eligibility and an operator’s commercial insurance are different topics. A covered patient trip does not establish that the operator’s business policy addresses every service it performs.

  2. Reviewing only the time spent driving

    Boarding, transferring assistance, securing equipment, and leaving the vehicle can introduce questions that are missed when the review starts and ends with a vehicle list.

  3. Treating a certificate as the whole policy

    A certificate summarizes selected information. Review the applicable forms and endorsements when a contract calls for a particular protection.

Frequently asked questions

What does NEMT insurance cover?

NEMT insurance is not one single coverage. A program may include commercial auto liability, physical damage, general liability, workers' compensation, umbrella or excess liability and other coverage depending on the operation.

Do wheelchair transportation companies need special insurance?

Wheelchair transportation introduces additional operational considerations including mobility equipment, vehicle modifications, passenger assistance, lifts, ramps and securement systems. These details should be discussed when reviewing coverage.

Does Medicaid require NEMT insurance?

Medicaid transportation programs and transportation brokers can establish provider and insurance requirements. Requirements vary by state, program and contract, so operators should review the specific requirements that apply to their business.

What should a new NEMT business review before its first trip?

Document the planned vehicles, passenger capacity, accessibility equipment, driver responsibilities, routes, and contracts. Compare those details with the policy and applicable provider requirements before service begins.

What documents help organize an NEMT insurance review?

Be prepared to provide information about vehicles, drivers, operating radius, passenger capacity, services, wheelchair accessibility, contracts, loss history and requested coverage limits.
